SECTION 2.1

Binomial expansion

We can express powers of (x + y) as follows:

(x+y)1 = x+y

(x+y)2 = x2 + 2xy + y2

(x+y)3 = x3 + 3x2y + 3xy2 + y3

(x+y)4 = x4+4 x 3y+ 6x2y 2+ 4xy3+y4

(x+y)5 =x 5+5 x4y+10 x 3y 2 + 10 x 2y 3+ 5xy4+y 5

Coefficients of the first five binomial powers
PowerCoefficients
(x+y)1 1 1
(x+y)2 1 2 1
(x+y)3 1 3 3 1
(x+y)4 1 4 6 4 1
(x+y)5 1 5 10 10 5 1

The coefficients in these expansions form Pascal's triangle, named after the French mathematician Blaise Pascal (1623–1662). The outer terms in each row are 1; each inner term is the sum of the two terms directly above it.

SECTION 2.2

The binomial theorem

The binomial coefficient nCr counts the number of r-element subsets of an n-element set. For example, consider the set X = {a, b, c, d}.

THEOREM 2.1

The binomial theorem

(x + y)n = ∑r=0n nCrxn-ryr

where n is a positive integer.

Special Case

(1 + x)n = 1 + nC1x + nC2x2 + ··· + nCn-1xn-1 + xn

Note.

For the expansion of (x+y)n, where n is a positive integer:

  1. The binomial coefficients are all integers.
  2. The coefficients of terms equidistant from the beginning and end of the expansion are equal.

That is

nC0 = nC n = 1

nC1 = nC n-1 = n

...

nCr = nC n-r

Corollary 2.2

The number of r-element subsets of an n-element set equals the number of (n-r)-element subsets:

7C5 = 7C2

15C10 = 15C5

Middle term in the expansion

In the expansion of (x+y)n, there are (n+1) terms.

  • If n is even, then (n+1) is odd, so ( n+2 2 ) th term will be the only one middle term in the expansion.
  • If n is odd, then (n+1) is even, so ( n+1 2 ) thterm and ( n+3 2 ) th term will be the two middle terms in the expansion.
